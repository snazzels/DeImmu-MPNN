ESTIMATOR: exact expectation of the best of k designs drawn from the
ensemble of 10, E[min of k] = sum_i x_(i) C(i-1,k-1)/C(n,k). Not a bootstrap.

Two selection rules are tabulated because they answer different questions.
  best-of-k : generate k, keep the single best. The natural ensemble-size curve.
  keep-3-of-k : generate k, keep the best 3 and average them. THIS is the rule
            behind the manuscript's published filtering baseline.

ACCEPTANCE TEST (unguarded, all 99 proteins, keep-3-of-10):
  recomputed 27.02%  vs published 26.60%  (delta +0.42 pp)  PASS

  fine-tuned (ensemble of 10) : mean 41.36%   pooled 43.50%
  fine-tuned, ONE design, no selection : mean 41.36%  (a single draw's
      expected burden is the ensemble mean, so no netMHCIIpan call is involved)

RESULT: filtering does NOT reach the fine-tuned reduction at any budget up to
        k=10. The largest budget tested reaches 36.36% against
        41.36%, a shortfall of 5.01 pp.
        The curve is monotone, so a crossing at some larger k is not excluded;
        what is excluded is a crossing within the budget range tested.

READ WITH TWO CAVEATS, both of which favour fine-tuning and neither of which
is captured by the crossing point alone:
  (a) COST IS NOT EQUAL AT EQUAL REDUCTION. Filtering at budget k requires k
      netMHCIIpan calls per protein at design time. The fine-tuned model
      requires none -- the objective is already in the weights.
  (b) THE ARMS ARE NOT LIKE FOR LIKE. The fine-tuned figure above is its own
      10-design ensemble mean; a single fine-tuned design (no selection at all)
      is the row marked k=1 in the fine-tuned line. The two strategies also
      compose -- filtering the FINE-TUNED ensemble is reported separately in
      the manuscript and exceeds either alone.
